# Web Hacking Techniques 2026 — AI-collected

> ## 🤖 Collected by an AI agent, not by the community
>
> **This file was assembled by an AI agent**, not by nominations, a community vote, or an
> expert panel. That is why it is named `2026-ai.md` and kept separate from the curated
> year lists (`2006.md` … `2025.md`), which are maintained by hand. Nothing here has been
> reviewed by a human, so treat every line as a lead to check rather than a settled fact.
>
> The known failure modes of a collection built this way: a link may be misattributed, a
> date may be wrong, the "novel" claim may not survive a real prior-art search, and the
> coverage is uneven — it is biased toward sources that publish in English on the open web
> and reachable without a login.
>
> ## ⚠️ Still a work in progress
>
> The 2026 voting round has not happened yet, and this covers only **1 January to 8 August
> 2026**. It is unranked and incomplete: several conferences fall later in the year, DEF CON
> 34 was still running when this was assembled, and the Watchlist below tracks work with no
> published artifact yet.
>
> Links point at the **original source**, not at news coverage of it. Where one piece of
> work has several artifacts, they are grouped on a single line.

## Watchlist — in scope but no primary artifact yet

Confirmed to exist and to fall in 2026, but with no slides, paper or writeup published as of
8 August 2026 — work that belongs on the list above once there is something citable to link.

-   **Cache Key Injection: Smuggling Poison Through the Door** — Alex Brumen (YesWeHack), Bug Bounty Village, delivered 7 August. Village decks are not mirrored on the DEF CON media server. `bugbountydefcon.com/presentations-2026` still 404s but that slug exists for DC32 and DC33, so it is the one to re-check; his blog `brum3ns.github.io` has not moved since October 2025 and no GitHub repo matches.
-   **This Message Was Sent by Microsoft: Turning Microsoft Apps into our Phishing Platform** — Keanu Nys (Spotit), DEF CON 34. Not on the media server; `insights.spotit.be` needs a browser user-agent to fetch and his newest post there is June 2024. His DEF CON 33 talk never got a writeup either, so this may simply not get one.
-   **New Hope for SSRF: Exploiting Credential Relay from APIM to AI Foundry** — Gyftos & Manousis, [Cloud Village DEF CON 34](https://www.cloud-village.org/dc34), scheduled 8 August so not yet delivered. One root cause across six Azure services: credentials and destination resolve independently, so user input reaches an outbound request carrying a Managed Identity token with no destination validation. CVE-2026-26118 assigned. Cloud Village historically pushes a slides repo within a week of the con.
-   **ROP for the Web: Compression Dictionaries** — Lenin Alevski, AppSec Village, scheduled 9 August so not yet delivered. It is an Arsenal slot, so the promised `gadget-scanner` tool will most likely drop with the talk; nothing exists publicly yet and his blog has been dormant since February 2025.
-   **Lost in Normalization: From URL Quirks to Poisoning the Azure Supply Chain** — Nir Ohfeld & Ronen Shustin (Wiz), Black Hat Asia 2026. `bh_files` is still empty months after the April event while 41 of 63 Asia-26 sessions have published theirs, and a scan of the entire Wiz blog RSS (663 items) found nothing matching. The affected service was retired after disclosure, so a writeup may never come.
-   **Pwning Agentic Browsers with PleaseFix** — Zenity Labs, Black Hat USA 2026. Re-checked 8 August: the research hub now documents real mechanisms for both subfamilies — Agent127 (steering the agent to localhost/127.0.0.1: reverse shell via Ollama+Open WebUI, pivots through Jupyter, pgAdmin) and HistoryFixing (planting fabricated browser-history entries the agent later trusts) — under the "Intent Collision" class name. But the Black Hat deck/whitepaper (`bh_files`) is still empty ("TBD"), and both constituent posts have already been assessed on their own.
-   **BUIzz: Finding Policy Enforcement Bugs via Interaction Simulation on the Browser User Interface** — Jung, Kim, Kim, Wi (UNIST), USENIX Security '26. Paper embargoed; the presentation page carries no PDF and the lab page lists it as "to appear" with no artifact. **Re-check 12 August**, when the embargo lifts. (It cleared 60 would be automatic once judged; at ≥60 it joins the list.)
-   Next.js July 2026 cache-key confusion on invalid UTF-8 request bodies (CVE-2026-64647) — vendor advisory only. The reporter is credited in the GitHub advisory as `yorukot`, whose blog carries nothing on it; every other hit is news restating the advisory.
-   Remaining DEF CON 34 days (8–9 August) and the whole of USENIX Security '26, Hexacon, POC, SAS and Nullcon Berlin still fall after this snapshot.

## Dropped from the Watchlist

Not deferred — these do not belong on a 2026 web list at all.

-   **Remote Server, Local Root. Welcome to MCP.** — Zhong, Wang, Liu, Guan, Black Hat Asia 2026. The research is **2025 work**: its primary artifact is an [Obsidian Security post from 30 September 2025](https://www.obsidiansecurity.com/blog/from-well-known-to-well-pwned-common-vulnerabilities-in-ai-agents), all five CVEs are 2025-numbered, and the MCP authorization spec was amended in November 2025 in response. Black Hat Asia 2026 was a later presentation of it.
-   **Writing to Shadow Stacks** — Tokarev, DEF CON 34. Linux kernel and CPU exploit-mitigation research: writing chosen values into CET shadow-stack memory via `/proc/self/mem` with `FOLL_FORCE`, `userfaultfd` on the shadow-stack VMA, and the user-mode `WRSSQ` instruction. No web, HTTP, browser or application-layer bearing.
